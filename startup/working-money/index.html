Working money: the cash that keeps you open

Profit is a number on paper. Cash is what pays the rent on the first of the month.

The gap

You buy stock before you sell it, and some customers pay late. In between, you must pay rent, wages and suppliers. The money that covers that gap is called working money.

A simple estimate

Add up your fixed monthly costs: rent, wages, power, instalments. Multiply by the number of months you think it will take to reach steady sales, usually at least three. Add the cost of the stock you must hold. That is a starting estimate; the worksheet does the first part.

A small example (made-up numbers)

Fixed costs of 30,000 a month for three months need 90,000 of working money. If you also keep 40,000 of stock, you need 130,000 before the first sale pays you anything back.

How to need less

  • Ask suppliers for a few days of credit, and sell for cash or advance where you can.
  • Keep stock small and fast-moving.
  • Keep fixed costs low at the start.

What to watch

Check cash weekly: money in the bank plus what customers owe, minus what you owe in the next month. If this number is falling, act early: chase payments, cut orders and talk to your supplier.

Common questions

How is working money different from profit?

Profit is sales minus costs over a period. Working money is the cash you need to keep going between paying for things and being paid.

How much should I keep?

Enough for the number of months you would take to recover from a slow start. Three months of fixed costs is a common minimum; estimate your own.
