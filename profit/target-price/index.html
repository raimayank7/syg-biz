Target price calculator

Instead of guessing a price, enter the margin you want and get the price that delivers it after every fee.

How the price is found

Profit changes in a straight line with price, so the calculator works it out exactly: it finds the price at which the profit divided by the sale value equals your target. With the example figures, the break-even price is ₹448.08 and a 25 percent margin needs ₹631.72.

Why not just add a margin to the cost?

Cost plus 25 percent ignores fees that grow with the price and fees that do not. A commission of 12 percent takes a share of every extra rupee, so to keep 25 percent you must sell at a price that is higher than cost times 1.25. This is also why a sale price that looks fine can lose money.

After you have the number

  • Round to a price buyers like and run the main calculator again.
  • Compare with competing listings. If you are far above them, cut a cost rather than the margin.
  • Check your price at the discount you plan for sale days: see discounts.

Common questions

Is margin or markup the better target?

Margin (profit as a share of the sale value) is what compares fairly across products. See the margin and markup page for the conversion.

Why is the target price higher than I expected?

Fees as a percentage of the price mean that each extra rupee of price keeps only part of it. A fixed fee and shipping must also be covered first, so the price needed for a margin is often well above cost plus the margin.
