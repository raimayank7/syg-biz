Wording an invitation people reply to

People respond to invitations that tell them everything in one glance and make replying easy.

What an invitation must say

  1. What the event is, and who is hosting.
  2. The day, date and start time, and how long it lasts.
  3. The place, with a map link.
  4. Anything to bring or wear, and whether children are welcome.
  5. How to reply, and by when.

Examples

You are warmly invited to the opening of Fresh Bake on Sunday, 13 December at 10 am, Shop 12, Model Town Market. Free cake slice for the first 100 guests. Please tell us if you will come: [link]

Dear [name], we are celebrating our 25th anniversary with a dinner on Saturday, 19 December at 8 pm at Green Lawn Banquet. We would be so happy to have you with us. Please reply by 14 December: [link]

Make replying easy

A link that opens a page with the details and a button "I will come" is easier than "reply to this message". The event page maker makes one, with a calendar file and a map link.

Tone

Write as you would speak to the guest. Use their name. Keep it short: two or three sentences before the details.

Respect privacy

Send invitations to people individually or in a group where everyone knows each other. Do not publish a list of who is coming.

Common questions

How far ahead should I invite people?

Two to three weeks for a big event, one week for a small one. Send a reminder two days before.

Should I ask for a reply?

Yes, with a date. Without one, you will not know the number until the day.
