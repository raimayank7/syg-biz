Freelance Rate

Hourly, fixed, retainer or value pricing: which suits you?

There are five common ways to charge. Each one moves the risk of a job overrunning to a different person.

ModelGood forMain riskProtect yourself by
HourlyShort or unclear tasks, support, work where the client changes things oftenThe client worries about hours; fast work earns lessEstimating a range, reporting hours weekly, setting a monthly cap
Day rateWorkshops, on-site work, steady engagementsHalf days and short days are argued overDefining a day (for example 8 hours) and a half-day rate
Fixed projectClear work with a defined result: a logo, a site, a reportScope grows, you absorb the extra timeWriting what is included, a revision limit and a price for extras
RetainerOngoing needs: content, support, assistanceThe client sends more work every monthA fixed number of hours, a rate for extra hours, a review every quarter
Value-basedWork with a clear money result for the clientHard to prove the result; clients may negotiate hardAgreeing the result in writing, a deposit and a floor price from your own rate

How to choose

Ask three questions. Is the work clearly defined? If yes, a fixed price is usually best. Will it continue? If yes, a retainer gives you steady income. Is the result worth far more to the client than your time? If yes, price on value, with your hourly floor underneath.

Mixing models

Many freelancers use more than one: a fixed price for a project, an hourly rate for changes after delivery, and a small monthly retainer for support. Write the rule for each part into the quote so the client knows which rate applies to which part of the work.

Whatever you choose, keep the floor

Whichever model you pick, compare the final price with the floor from the rate calculator. A fixed price of 40,000 for a job that takes 80 hours is a rate of 500 an hour; the calculator tells you if that is enough. Use the project price calculator to turn hours into a quote with a buffer.

Common questions

Which model do clients prefer?

Many clients like fixed prices because they know the cost, but they also like to know what is included. A fixed price with a clear scope and a rate for extras is usually well accepted.

Can I charge by value if I am new?

You can, but it is harder because you have little proof of results. Start with fixed prices based on your floor and move to value pricing as you collect results to show.
