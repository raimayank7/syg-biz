Freelance quote and payment wording to copy

A good quote protects both sides. These lines are our own plain wording; change them to fit your work, and ask a lawyer to review anything that matters.

This is not legal advice and not a contract template for every country. For a large project, have a lawyer in your country review your terms.

Scope

Scope: [describe the result in one or two sentences]. This quote includes [list]. It does not include [list]. Work outside this scope will be quoted separately.

Revisions

This price includes [2] rounds of changes on the same brief. Further changes are charged at [rate] per hour. A change to the brief itself counts as new work.

Deposit and payment dates

A deposit of [percentage] is due before work starts. The balance is due [number] days after delivery. Work starts when the deposit is received.

Late payment

Invoices unpaid after [number] days may be followed by a late payment charge of [amount or percentage], if allowed by law, and new work may be paused until the invoice is paid.

Cancellation

If the project is cancelled, work completed up to that date is paid for at the quoted rate and the deposit is not refunded for work already done.

Ownership and use

Ownership of the final work passes to the client when the invoice is paid in full. Until then the work remains with the creator. The creator may show the work in a portfolio unless the client asks in writing for it to be kept private.

Validity

This quote is valid for [number] days. After that the price may change.

Common questions

How long should my quote be?

One page is enough for most freelance jobs: what you will do, what it costs, when it is due and how you are paid. Long legal text is better kept for a contract on larger projects.

Do I need a signed contract for a small job?

A written quote that the client accepts by email is useful proof for small jobs. For larger or riskier work, a signed contract reviewed by a lawyer is safer.
