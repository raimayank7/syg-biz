A no-show and cancellation policy that customers accept

A customer who does not turn up costs you the slot and the money it would have earned. A few simple habits cut no-shows without upsetting anyone.

Four habits that work

  1. Send a reminder the evening before and ask for a "YES". Many no-shows are people who forgot.
  2. Make cancelling easy. People who can cancel with one message do so, and you can offer the slot to someone else.
  3. Take an advance for long or costly slots. Money paid makes people remember.
  4. Say the rule in writing when you confirm.

Wording to copy

Please tell us at least [4 hours] before if you cannot come, so we can offer your time to someone else. If you do not come and do not tell us, the advance is kept / a fee of [amount] is added to your next visit.

If you are more than [15 minutes] late, we may have to shorten the service or move it to another day.

Your advance of [amount] is adjusted against the final bill. If you cancel [24 hours] before, it is returned in full.

How to apply it kindly

Use the rule the first time you meet a customer, not after a missed visit. When someone breaks it, ask what happened. A customer who had an emergency should not be charged; a customer who always forgets can be asked for an advance next time. Decide beforehand what you will do, so you are not deciding in an awkward moment.

Do not over-punish

A harsh rule loses customers. Most small businesses lose more from empty slots than from a few late cancellations, so the aim is to win the slot back, not to punish. A good habit is to keep a short list of regulars who would like a last-minute slot, so a cancelled time can be filled the same day.

Check local rules

Rules on advances, refunds and consumer rights differ by country. Check what applies where you work before you set a fee for no-shows.

Common questions

How long before should I ask customers to cancel?

It depends on how easily you can fill a slot. For a haircut a few hours is fine; for a long bridal appointment a day or more is fair.

Should I charge a fee for a first-time no-show?

Many businesses do not, and instead remind and offer another time. Use the fee for repeat no-shows.
