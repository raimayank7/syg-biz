Pricing tiffin and subscription plans

A plan sells regular income, and the price must still cover a day when a customer does not eat.

Start from the cost of one meal

Add the cost of ingredients, gas, packing, delivery and your time, and divide by the meals you make. That is your cost per meal. Do this once a month because prices change. Then decide the profit you want on each meal, which gives the price of one meal.

The example gives about 6 percent off the weekly plan and 8 percent off the monthly plan. A longer plan earns a bigger discount because it saves you the work of finding customers and chasing payments. Check the discount against your margin before you offer it: the discount calculator shows how many extra sales a price cut needs.

Holidays and skipped days

Decide the rule before the first plan: for example, "two skipped days a month with a day's notice are added to the end of the plan". Without a rule, every skipped day is an argument. The book shows the end date of each plan so you can add days when you agree to a skip.

Take payment first

Ask for payment at the start of the plan. It stops unpaid plans and gives you money to buy ingredients. The book shows each unpaid balance.

Common questions

Should I give a free trial meal?

Many sellers do for the first day. Count it in your costs and tell the customer it is a trial.

What if prices of ingredients go up?

Review your prices every month and tell customers a month before a change. Plans already paid keep the old price until they end.
