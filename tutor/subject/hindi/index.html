Teaching Hindi as a tutor

A simple weekly plan for your Hindi, and a way to keep track of progress.

How a class can run

Spend time on reading aloud, spelling and matras, then writing a short paragraph or letter, then grammar through examples from the student's own writing.

A sample week

  • Monday: reading aloud and matras
  • Tuesday: new words and meanings
  • Wednesday: grammar through examples
  • Thursday: writing a letter or paragraph
  • Friday: dictation and a short review

What to track for each student

Track reading fluency, matra and spelling errors, vocabulary and the type of sentence mistakes. Write one line a week in the notebook or in your head, and bring it into the monthly progress note.

Homework at home

Ask for reading a page aloud and copying five new words with their meanings.

Tests

Use dictation and a short paragraph or letter each month.

A common mistake

Teaching grammar rules without examples; the student remembers the rule but cannot use it.

Fees and records

Set the fee from your hours, including preparation and marking (see setting fees), and record each student in the manager with the subject in the class name, for example "Hindi, Class 9".

Common questions

How many students should one batch have in Hindi?

Choose a number where you can still see each student's work. Smaller groups let you correct more; larger groups bring more income but need a clear routine.

How do I show progress to parents?

Keep the weekly line and the monthly test, then send a short note. See the progress notes page for wording.
