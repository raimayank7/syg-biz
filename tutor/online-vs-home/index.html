Online or in-person tuition: which should you offer?

Many tutors now teach both. Each has costs and benefits that are easy to miss.

PointIn personOnline
ReachStudents nearby onlyAnyone with internet
Travel and roomTime and cost, or a room at homeNone
EquipmentA board, books, printoutsA device, a good connection, a headset, a digital board or a camera over paper
AttentionEasy to see if a student is lostHarder, so you ask more questions
Young childrenBetter for handwriting and focusNeeds a parent nearby
SafetyKeep the door open or a parent nearUse platforms where parents can join; record only with permission

Fees

Online classes remove your travel, but not your preparation or marking. Do not discount heavily just because it is online. Work out your hourly floor with the Freelance Rate calculator and compare.

Making online classes work

  • Test the connection and tools before each class.
  • Keep a second way to reach the family, such as a phone call, if the connection fails.
  • Share notes after class so students do not have to copy from the screen.
  • Keep classes shorter, with a change every ten to fifteen minutes.

A mix

A common path: start in person with neighbours, then add online classes for students further away or in exam season. Use the same manager for both; put "online" in the batch name.

Common questions

Can I record online classes?

Only with the knowledge and permission of the parents, and for a clear reason, such as students who missed a class. Tell them how long you keep the recording and who can see it.

Which platform should I use?

Choose one that students already know and that works on a phone, since many students use phones. Test it with one family first.
