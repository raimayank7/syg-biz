Seller Profit

What a discount really costs

A price cut shaves your margin on every unit. The extra sales needed to cover it are bigger than most sellers think.

The formula

If your margin per unit is m percent of the old price and you cut the price by d percent, the margin per unit falls to (m - d). To earn the same total profit you must sell m / (m - d) times as many units, which is d / (m - d) more. A 10 percent discount on a 30 percent margin needs 50 percent more sales.

Better ways to attract buyers

  • Offer a gift or free shipping above a basket value instead of a cut.
  • Sell a bundle with a small saving.
  • Give a code only to repeat customers.
  • Cut a cost you can control, then pass part of the saving on.

Common questions

Does a discount always bring enough extra sales?

No. At a 20 percent margin, a 10 percent discount needs sales to double just to earn the same profit. Most sellers never get that lift from a small price cut.

When is a discount worth giving?

When you can lower a cost at the same time, clear stock that would otherwise be thrown away, or win customers who return, or when a big event will bring volume you could not get otherwise.
