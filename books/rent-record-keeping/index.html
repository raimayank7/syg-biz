Keeping rent records as a small landlord

Disputes about rent usually come from missing records. A few lines each month make them rare.

What to record every month

  • Tenant and place: the name and the flat, room or shop.
  • Month and rent due.
  • Amount received and the date, and how it was paid.
  • Anything that changes the amount: a part payment, a deduction you both agreed, a repair the tenant paid for.

Write it the day you receive the money

Open the rent book, find the row, and write the amount and the date. A bank transfer or UPI leaves its own record, which you can match to your entry at month end. Cash needs a written receipt: the NS rent receipt maker makes one in a minute.

Deposits and agreements

Keep a note of the deposit amount and the date, and keep the written agreement in a safe place. The rules for deposits, notice and increases differ by country and state and change, so this site does not state them. Ask a lawyer or the local authority where you live.

A monthly routine

  1. On the first of the month, copy last month's tenants into the new month.
  2. On the fifth, send a reminder to those who have not paid.
  3. At month end, add up what you received and what is still due.
  4. Download a backup once a month.

When a tenant pays late

Be polite and specific: say the month and the amount. Use the reminder message in the rent book. Note each late payment in the row, so that you have the facts if you ever need to talk about the pattern.

Taxes

Rent is income, and rules about showing it and about deductions differ by country. Keep your records tidy and ask an accountant what to report.

Common questions

Should I give a receipt for every payment?

It is good practice, and for cash it matters. A receipt protects both you and the tenant.

How long should I keep rent records?

At least as long as tax rules in your country require, which an accountant can tell you.
